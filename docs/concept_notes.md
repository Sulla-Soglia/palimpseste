## The Troy section (Z., 2026-07-07)

Proposed by Zhihan while listening to Por una cabeza: the Troy excavation
cross-section (Schliemann's trench; Troy I–IX stacked into one vertical cut,
~4,000 years — the canonical image of archaeological stratigraphy, displayed
at the Istanbul Archaeology Museums) as the third term of the project's
central analogy.

Three regimes of strata, three regimes of reading:

| | Troy | Alsace facade | Model weights |
|---|---|---|---|
| Geometry of time | vertical: time = depth | lateral: periods coexist on one visible surface | superposed: strata share the same parameters |
| Layer separation | physical, ordered | interleaved (German roof, French windows, hybrid door) | none — distributed |
| How to read | destructive excavation (one cut, one look) | walk past it; it faces the street | probes / attention maps |

The distinction this sharpens: **stratigraphy vs palimpsest.** In a
stratigraphy the old layer lies *under* the new; in a palimpsest the old
layer shows *through* the new, on the same surface. Alsace buildings are
palimpsests wearing stratigraphic history.

The methodological claim that follows: if Grad-CAM attention maps decompose
a facade into cultural-temporal layers (RQ2), the model performs a
**non-destructive section** — it produces the Troy cut without moving a
brick. A virtual Schliemann's trench, read off the surface. And the same
instrument aimed back at the model itself is Training Stratigraphy
(arXiv: 2605.28102): weights, too, are strata that can be sectioned only
virtually.

Paper placement: this belongs in the framing (intro or discussion), as the
figure logic — ideally a three-panel figure: Troy section / annotated facade
/ Grad-CAM map, same caption grammar for all three.
